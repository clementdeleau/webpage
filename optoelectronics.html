Photonic components & exotic materials

From single photons emitted by carbon nanotubes at room temperature to sensors that read refractive index and displacement on a chip: making light do measurable, useful work.

Room-temperature single photons on a chip

Functionalized carbon nanotubes can emit light one photon at a time, even at room temperature. At RIKEN I integrate these emitters into photonic cavities and waveguide circuits, so that single photons are generated, enhanced and routed on the same chip.

The quantum character of the light is checked with photon-correlation measurements after it has travelled through the circuit, a key step toward practical, scalable quantum photonics.

Refractometric & gas sensors

During my PhD I developed long-period waveguide gratings in silicon nitride: periodic structures that couple light between guided modes and turn tiny changes in the surrounding refractive index into large spectral shifts.

Width-modulated and rib-waveguide designs pushed the sensitivity, a scaling study showed how far the approach can go, and the same devices were used for gas sensing. The thesis received three awards.

Optical feedback interferometry & displacement

Optical feedback interferometry turns a laser into a displacement sensor. I moved the FM-to-AM conversion at its heart onto photonic integrated circuits, including micro-ring resonators, toward compact sensors resolving nanometre-scale displacements.
