Clément Deleau

Researcher in integrated photonics and optoelectronics
Carbon nanotube single-photon sources on photonic chips

I design, fabricate and characterize photonic integrated circuits on silicon nitride and lithium niobate, and bring exotic materials onto the chip for applications ranging from quantum communication circuits with carbon-nanotube single-photon sources to ultra-sensitive gas and displacement sensors and on-chip modulation for spectrometry. I also develop automated optoelectronic setups, perform quantum photonics and light-matter interaction experiments, and train neural networks to understand and manipulate light spectra.

about

Engineer by training, photonics researcher by passion

I am a postdoctoral researcher at RIKEN (Japan), specializing in integrated photonics, nanophotonics and optoelectronics, with a particular focus on photonic integrated circuits and their applications.

I graduated in engineering (Physics Engineering) from INSA Toulouse and obtained my PhD in 2022 at LAAS-CNRS, Université de Toulouse. After postdoctoral research at Toulouse INP (ENSEEIHT), I joined the Quantum Optoelectronics Research Team of Dr. Yuichiro Kato at the RIKEN Center for Advanced Photonics as a JSPS Postdoctoral Fellow, and I now hold a RIKEN Special Postdoctoral Researcher fellowship.

My research covers the design, fabrication and experimental characterization of integrated photonic devices on silicon and silicon nitride, with a strong focus on integrating exotic materials and actively controlling light-matter interaction. During my PhD, I developed new types of diffractive and interferometric waveguide devices and combined them with porous functionalized polymers for chemical sensing. I then developed high-quality integrated resonators and photonic circuits for remote vibration sensors that resolve nanometre-scale displacements.

I have implemented Pockels-effect polymers in modulators, enabling photonic control through both electro-optic and thermo-optic effects, and integrated functionalized carbon-nanotube emitters into nanophotonic cavities, achieving single-photon emission at room temperature. My current research focuses on novel integrated spectroscopic systems, nanolasers, and photonic control by training artificial neural networks. Along the way I've built expertise in nanofabrication, optical characterization, photonic device design and experimental instrumentation in international, multidisciplinary teams.

Three ways I work with light

01

Photonic components & light-matter interaction

On-chip components and how light interacts with nanomaterials and exotic materials: carbon-nanotube single photons in resonant cavities, electro-optic ring modulators, gas, refractometric and displacement sensors.

02

Nanofabrication & process development

Building photonic integrated circuits on silicon nitride and lithium niobate in the cleanroom: deposition, e-beam lithography, plasma etching and metallization, from layout to tested chip.

03

AI, signals & chip control

AI-based signal processing, and control of the modulation signals sent into photonic chips for spectral generation and measurement, with neural networks trained on supercomputers.
