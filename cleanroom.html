Nanofabrication & process development

I take photonic devices from idea to working chip: simulation and layout, nanofabrication in the cleanroom, then optical characterization on the bench.

Platforms I work with

Silicon nitride offers low-loss waveguides with a wide transparency window. I have used it for long-period grating sensors, micro-ring resonators, tunable filters and the waveguide circuits that carry single photons from carbon nanotubes.

Lithium niobate brings a strong electro-optic effect, ideal for fast, voltage-tunable resonators and integrated spectroscopic systems.

From layout to chip

A typical device goes through these steps, iterated until the measured response matches the design.

  1. DesignMode and propagation simulations, mask layout
  2. LithographyPatterning waveguides, gratings and rings
  3. EtchingTransferring patterns into the photonic layer
  4. DepositionCladdings, heaters and electrodes
  5. DicingSeparating dies and preparing facets
  6. TestingFiber coupling, spectra, imaging

Process engineering

Much of the work is process engineering: tuning recipes so that widths, depths and periods come out as designed, since a few nanometres change a grating or resonator response. My PhD and postdoc devices were fabricated with the micro and nanotechnology teams at LAAS-CNRS in Toulouse. My current devices are being fabricated at RIKEN and in the cleanroom of the University of Tokyo.
